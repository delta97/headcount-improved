# One-on-one prep

## The failure this prevents

The agenda block gets written fresh each cycle from memory, so the items that surface are the
recent ones, not the important ones — and the follow-up promised in the last 1:1 is exactly the
kind of thing memory drops. The person across the table notices.

## Sources

1. **The last 1:1 notes with this person** — commitments made in both directions are the first
   candidates for this agenda. An unkept one leads.
2. **Weekly notes pages since then** — every mention of the person: items assigned to them,
   items waiting on them, decisions involving their area.
3. **The open-items rollup** — anything tiered urgent that touches their workstream.
4. **Structured meeting notes** where they were present or on the action-items table.

## Structure

The established three-part agenda, written as the owner writes it:

1. **New priorities** — what changed since last time that they need to know or weigh in on.
2. **Also working on** — status lines on the standing threads between you; one line each,
   state and next step. This is where "still waiting on X from you" lives, stated plainly.
3. **Questions** — the decisions or inputs needed from them, phrased so each can be answered
   in the meeting. A question that needs prework gets sent ahead, not sprung.

## Method

1. Confirm who and when; find the last 1:1 page for the baseline.
2. Harvest and sort mentions into the three sections. Attribute carefully — an item is theirs,
   yours, or jointly pending, and the agenda should show which.
3. Trim to what one meeting holds: five to eight items. Everything else goes to a "parked"
   list at the bottom rather than silently vanishing, so next cycle starts from it.
4. Write the block into the current weekly notes page under a timestamped heading in the
   owner's convention ("2026-08-18 Brijesh One on One"), ready to be reordered by hand.

## Rules

- Adjust register to the relationship: the manager 1:1 leads with asks and risks; a peer or
  report 1:1 leads with their blockers and what you owe them.
- Never carry a sensitive item (compensation, performance, personnel) into an agenda page
  others might see; flag it as "one verbal item" instead.
